## Problem

A parent's nomination that a write creates is always unconfirmed: `private.parent_nomination_resolved` inserts the party's (or alliance's) bare row and nothing else, for `set_entity_nominations` and for `create_invitations` alike. The child's own confirmation is the caller's: an invitation states `confirmed: true` on a candidate's nomination, and `p_confirm_entity = true` confirms the candidate, in the same call that creates the party's nomination unconfirmed. An administrator could produce the same state before Phase 177.1 by writing the rows directly; the invitation makes it one call.

The state that results: a confirmed candidate, with a confirmed nomination, whose `parent_nomination_id` names a party nomination that is not confirmed.

**Inferred from the policies, not run:** `anon_select_nominations` (`302-rls.sql`) admits a nomination when the project is open for voters, the row is confirmed and every entity it links is confirmed. The candidate's nomination passes; the party's does not. So an anonymous voter reads a candidate nomination whose parent nomination it cannot read.

**Not run by anyone:** what the voter app then shows. Whether the candidate is listed in the results, under which party or as an independent, whether the party appears in the party list, the party filter or the candidate's card, and whether the data model's nomination tree (`@openvaa/data`) accepts a child whose parent nomination is absent or raises.

## What would show it

- A dataset or a spec that holds this state in an open project: a confirmed candidate with a confirmed nomination under a party nomination left unconfirmed, beside a candidate of the same party whose parent nomination is confirmed.
- As `anon`, the rows `get_nominations` returns for that constituency, and the parent each one names.
- In a browser, the voter results list, the candidate's card and entity page, the party list and the party filter, compared between the two candidates.

What to do with the answer is a separate decision. The options the phase deferred stay deferred: confirming the parent's nomination together with the child's (177.1-C2 (b)), or refusing the mixed state (177.1-C2 (c)).

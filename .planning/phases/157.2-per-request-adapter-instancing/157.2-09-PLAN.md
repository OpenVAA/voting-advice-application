<objective>
Close the class with a mechanism, prove the mechanism fires, and close the phase on one honest gate.

**Purpose.** Criterion D11-6 is the only one of the six about the future: a test must fail when a new
server-side selector is added as a module singleton. Ruling D11 is explicit that what is being retired is the
class rather than its instances, and everything plans 02 through 08 did is undone by one new module-scope
instance unless something mechanical says no. **Decision D1(a)** picks the mechanism and the repository has two
precedents of exactly this shape: an ESLint syntax ban declared inline in the frontend flat config, locked in
by a spec that lints virtual fixtures through the **real** config.

**This plan carries four tasks rather than the usual three, deliberately.** The guard's blind half, its
installation and its lock-in are the triple 157.1 proved cannot be collapsed — a blindness measured after
installation is not a measurement of blindness — and the phase gate has to run after them, in the same plan,
because the phase has no tenth plan. Splitting the triple would be worse than the size.

**Decision D2's NOTE asked a question and research answered it with measurements; the answer ships.** Both
halves of the ban are kept. The second half is re-expressed as a **declaration** ban rather than a call ban,
for two measured reasons. The call form fires on two live in-tree sites that survive this phase untouched — a
store method and its spec, neither anything to do with adapters — so shipping it means shipping two suppression
comments at sites the rule was never about, which teaches the next reader that the rule is noise. And "keep only
the instance clause" concedes the one thing removing the method cannot prevent: nothing stops a contributor
re-adding the method next quarter, and the instance clause is silent on that. A declaration ban is live after
the removal, has a permanent target in the self-test's fixtures, and was measured with zero false positives.

**The declaration clause needs two forms, and which form goes in which block is a measurement, not a taste.**
Executed during planning against fixtures and against the real files: the broad form fires on the abstract base,
on the mixin's inner class, **and on the live store method** — a false positive. The class-name-scoped form
fires on the abstract base, is silent on the store, and is also silent on the mixin's inner class, whose name
carries no adapter suffix. The abstract base and the store resolve to the **same** effective zone, so they
cannot be separated by block placement. Therefore the scoped form goes into all three blocks, and the broad form
goes into the adapter block only — where there is no non-adapter class for it to fire on, and where it is the
only form that reaches the mixin.

**The replacement trap is the reason the shape of this edit is spread rather than appended.** Per file and per
rule, a later matching object's options array replaces an earlier one's entirely. Measured across six paths at
this HEAD, three distinct effective arrays exist: two entries, four entries and seven entries. A new fourth
object scoped anywhere useful would delete between two and seven inherited restrictions for the files it
matches, **and produce zero errors doing it**. Extending an array inside an object that already exists is
additive and triggers no replacement, so nothing needs re-declaring byte-identically and no new regression can
be introduced — but the standing regressions are still written, one per inherited entry per zone, because they
are the only detector that exists.
</objective>

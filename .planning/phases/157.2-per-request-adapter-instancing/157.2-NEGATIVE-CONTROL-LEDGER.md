## Why this ledger exists — and what it does NOT claim

The standing milestone rule, quoted through `157.1-NEGATIVE-CONTROL-LEDGER.md` from `REQUIREMENTS.md:7-13`:

> **prove the guard fails before claiming it guards** — negative control run twice, once against the old
> assertion to demonstrate blindness, once against the new one to demonstrate the catch.

A blindness measurement taken *after* the subject moved is not a measurement of blindness. That is why this
register is opened in wave 1, before `157.2-02` touches the first application file: **every OLD half is
scheduled in the plan that is about to move that row's subject**, which is the only ordering under which the
OLD half is a measurement of the pre-change tree rather than a claim about it. Once a subject moves, its OLD
half can never be taken again.

**What this ledger does NOT claim.**

1. It does not claim any behaviour is fixed. At `157.2-01` close, nothing is fixed; three spec files and this
   document are the entire output of wave 1.
2. It does not claim the five rows exhaust the phase's verification. They are the **negative controls**; the
   positive assertions live in the specs and in `157.2-VALIDATION.md`'s criterion map, and the phase's closing
   gate is `157.2-CONTEXT.md` § **E2(a)**.
3. It does not claim the guard rows prove the defect class is closed by construction.
   `157.2-RESEARCH.md` § "What the guard cannot see" measured two forms no esquery selector reaches — lazy
   module-level memoization and a renamed class. Rows 4 and 5 are a lock against *reopening the measured
   shape*, not a proof of impossibility; rows 1 and 2 are the backstop for both evasions, because neither
   evasion can produce a per-request instance and neither can therefore pass the interleaving assertions.

## Schema each row carries

Each row records five fields and nothing else until its owner measures it:

`Owner` · `Subject files` · `OLD-half command` · `NEW-half command` · `Expected`, followed by a measurement
table whose every cell reads `pending` until the owning plan fills it. A filled cell carries its own command,
exit code, output tail, HEAD and log path.

**Cells are filled only by the plan named in `Owner`.** A neighbouring row's observation is not evidence about
this row, and an inference from a sibling cell is not a measurement of this cell.

## Row 1 — `#supabase` sharing: request A resumes and observes request B's client

- **Owner:** `157.2-02` (both halves).
- **Subject files:** `apps/frontend/src/lib/api/adapters/supabase/supabaseAdapter.ts` (the `#supabase` field
  and the `init` override at `:23,27-48`), `apps/frontend/src/lib/api/adapters/supabase/dataProvider/index.ts`
  (the module singleton), `apps/frontend/src/lib/api/dataProvider.ts` (the selector the seam imports).
- **Instance class:** `157.2-CONTEXT.md` § **C1(a)** and § **E1(a)**, "the concurrency test"; criterion **C1**
  and **C2** in `157.2-VALIDATION.md`.
- **OLD-half command:** the `#supabase` interleaving in
  `apps/frontend/src/lib/api/adapters/supabase/supabaseAdapter.concurrency.test.ts`, run while `obtain()` binds
  to the module singleton plus `init()` — which is how all eighteen call sites reach an adapter today. The
  interleaving is `configure A → park A → configure B → complete B → resume A`, and request A then reads back a
  tagged value. At HEAD the tag returned to A is **`B`**, so the isolation assertion must be observed RED.
- **NEW-half command:** the **same spec file, unchanged**, with `obtain()`'s one-line body flipped to the
  per-call factory `createDataProvider(config)` that `157.2-02` adds. That flip is the entire difference
  between the two runs; if any other line of the spec differs, the GREEN does not answer the RED.
- **Expected:** OLD — RED, request A observes tag `B`. NEW — GREEN, request A observes tag `A`.

**Why the seam is a single named function, recorded here rather than in the spec alone.** The threat this row
carries is not that the fix fails; it is that the RED and the GREEN quietly become two different tests
(`157.2-01-PLAN.md` § threat register, **T-157.2-01**). The mitigation is structural: `obtain()` is the only
construction site in the spec file, its body is one expression, and every case reaches the adapter through it.

**Why a single method call could not have produced this row.** `getAppSettings` reads `this.supabase`
**synchronously before its first `await`**, so an interleave *inside* one adapter method cannot show the bleed.
The defect lives at the call site, and fact 7 records its shape:
`init` (`candidate/(protected)/+layout.server.ts:30`) → `await locals.safeGetSession()` (`:33`) →
`await dataWriter.getCandidateUserData(…)` (`:44`). The spec therefore models **two adapter operations per
logical request with the request's own `await` between them**.

**Why this vector is live even though no Supabase adapter calls `this.fetch` itself — and why the ORIGINAL
justification for it is wrong.** Fact 5 as written argues that SvelteKit's request-scoped `fetch` forwards
that request's cookies to Supabase, so a shared `#fetch` leaks credentials. **Amendment finding C measured that
premise as false**: SvelteKit's load `fetch` forwards cookies **same-domain only**, Supabase is cross-origin,
and PostgREST authenticates on `Authorization` rather than on cookies. The comment at `supabaseAdapter.ts:40`
asserting otherwise is ruling **D10**'s root cause written down in prose. Fact 5's *conclusion* nevertheless
survives, by a different route: `SupabaseDataWriter` inherits `clearIdToken`, `logout` and
`exchangeCodeForIdToken` from `UniversalDataWriter` **un-overridden** (`clearIdToken` at
`universalDataWriter.ts:106-110` reaches `/api/oidc/token`), and those are **same-origin app routes**, where
the forwarded cookie is exactly the request's own session. **Any design resting on "the fetch forwards the
cookies to Supabase" is resting on the defect**; this row rests on the same-origin inheritance instead.

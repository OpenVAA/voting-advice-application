<objective>
Discharge the one durability concern the rename raises. `external_id` is a stable key that both the
bulk upsert and the teardown filter depend on, so a rename can strand rows on any database that is not
reset first. Prove that it does not, by producing the strand deliberately and then measuring that
teardown reaches it — and finish by confirming the renamed dataset still renders in the app.

Purpose: D-05 rated this rename **costly** precisely because stranding was plausible and unproven. The
proof is four counts and a teardown, and it is cheap. The alternative — asserting that the unchanged
prefix keeps teardown's reach intact — is exactly the kind of unmeasured claim this milestone exists
to stop accepting.

Output: ledger rows `S1`…`S4`, a `## Strand proof` section, and a green app-level probe run against
the renamed seed.
</objective>

<polarity>
## ⚠ In `145-07`, step 2 is expected to look WRONG — that is the measurement

- `S1` — after seeding the old idiom on a reset database, the counts are the template's declared sizes
  and every identifier carries the **old** typecode. Expected: 8 organizations, 5 constituencies.
- `S2` — after seeding the renamed template **without a reset**, the counts are expected to
  **double**: 16 organizations, 10 constituencies, half in each idiom. ⚠ This is the strand, made
  visible on purpose. A run that shows 8 and 5 here means the second seed matched and replaced the
  first, which would mean the identifiers did not actually change — re-derive.
- `S3` — after teardown, expected **0 rows of either idiom**. ⚠ A non-zero count here is the failure
  the whole proof exists to detect, and it would mean the rename **does** strand rows. Record it and
  stop; do not proceed to step 4 as though the proof passed.
- `S4` — after a full reset and re-seed, back to 8 and 5, all new idiom, no residue.

The projections above are projections. **If a count differs, record the observed number** and
investigate the difference rather than adjusting the expectation.
</polarity>

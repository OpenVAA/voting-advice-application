## T-179.1-163 and T-179.1-165

The WR16G audit opened T-163 on its review's WR-01 and WR-03, and T-165 on CR-01, WR-02 and the first form of WR-01. Plans 67 and 68 removed every one of those causes, by fix or by the operator's acceptance.

**T-163 closes.** Its threat is a walked-away user's session outliving a closed, frozen or locked tab. Three narrow paths still reach it: Duplicate Tab or a full-load navigation of the counting tab, two modals counting at once, and a planted deadline. Each comes from a mechanism this round introduced, so this audit opens them under the round's rows, T-180 and T-182, and not twice.

**T-165 stays open, on the review's WR-01, which this audit confirmed by reading.** At `699638bda`, `clearLogoutDeadline()` expired the cookie whoever wrote it, so the immediate Log out in another tab (`button:116`) removed an orphaned count. Plan 67's next document did the same. Plan 68 returns early in a document with no count of its own (`deadline:119`), and removes only its own id (`:121`). The Admin App's Log out and every server-side sign-out never touch the cookies. So:

1. A tab closes during its count.
2. The user signs out at once in another tab, and the count stays `'counting'`.
3. The user, or the next user of a shared device, signs in before the deadline drops.
4. The next request after the drop is `'runOut'`. The hook signs out the new session (`hook:94`), and the settlement discards that session's drafts.

A user who is present is signed out once, within at most the count plus ten seconds of the tab's close. That is the row's threat.

`AR-179.1-15` does not cover it. Its rationale says a closed tab's count does "as a count left running in an open tab does". An open tab follows a sign-out made elsewhere to the login page (`context:442-445,616-622`). That navigation closes the modal (`button:99-104`), and the close withdraws the count (`button:133`). So with the tab open, the later sign-in keeps its session.

The review's fix (withdraw every count on any sign-out or sign-in the application makes) would close this path. Choosing it is the operator's call.

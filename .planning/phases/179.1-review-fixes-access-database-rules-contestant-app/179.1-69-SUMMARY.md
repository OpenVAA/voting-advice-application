## For the operator's reading

**What a user now meets.** A tab that met a deadline sign-out through a client-side request can sign in again in that same tab, and the session stays. No clock disagreement between the device and the server moves the deadline, because the deadline is the expiry the browser keeps for `logout-deadline`. A page opened in another tab during a count leaves the count alone; only the tab that held the modal removes it. Log out is browser-wide, as you ruled at 179.1-67 (`a`). So closing the tab that holds a counting modal ends the session in every tab of that browser at its next request, and removes their drafts, as a count left running does. That is `AR-179.1-15`.

**What a production build proved, and what it did not.** In a production build (`179.1-69-prod-green`), the six titles of the round all passed on the two cookies: the four new titles of plans 67 and 68, and plan 63's two (`a count left in a tab that closes ends the session` and `a count whose timer did not run signs out`). The manifest shows a server load on every entry route. Every drive is Chromium on one origin. No run covers WebKit or Firefox, a real closed browser, Safari's cap on a cookie a page writes, a device whose operating-system clock is off, a cookie planted for a parent domain, two modals counting at once, the identity provider's callback or the Admin App's sign-in after a deadline sign-out, or a page restored from the back-forward cache with its modal counting. Each is on the list below.

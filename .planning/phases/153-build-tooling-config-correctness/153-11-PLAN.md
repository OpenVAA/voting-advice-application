## The two things most likely to go wrong

**1. Narrowing a gate is how a gate stops working.** Three of this milestone's sharpest findings were
gates that reported clean zeros while blind — a scan printing `bare = 0 … OK` over 40 live
violations, gate blindness measured between 27% and 72% across seven partitions, and `git grep -E`
returning zero hard-coded ports repo-wide over a file whose line 10 read `port = 54321`. **Every
narrowing in this plan must be paired with a flip test proving the row still catches what it should.**
A `task-id` row that no longer matches `D-06` is not narrowed, it is broken.

**2. Order is load-bearing for D7b.** Adding `css`/`scss` to the family map before `app.css` is swept
enables a rule against 19 pre-existing violations. That guard cannot go live until the sweep runs, so
it would have to ship disabled — and a disabled guard is no guard. Sweep first. Always.

Purpose: convert three answered questions into a tree and a gate that agree.
Output: a narrowed gate, a swept `app.css`, two codified vendored exclusions, `css`/`scss` covered,
and an honest disposition of the two requirements.
</objective>

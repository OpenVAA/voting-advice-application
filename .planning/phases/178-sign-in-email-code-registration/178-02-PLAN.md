## Rulings and deviations carried by this plan

1. **The probe is run first, on the local stack** (the orchestrator's rule 4). Each later plan that rests on a row names it and says what it does if the row is not held; the table is in the execution rules.
2. **P7 is measured at the auth service, not through the sign-in screen's action** (item PL-16). The action adds no step before the service's own password sign-in, which plan 178-13 pins with a unit case; the research asks for thirty attempts per case, and the local stack admits thirty password sign-ins in five minutes from one address of origin, so the probe makes **nine per case — twenty-seven sign-ins**, three under that limit. Nine is the number the execution rules' item PL-16 gives. The stack's two limits are separate — thirty sign-ins, and thirty link verifications, each in five minutes — and the probe budgets each on its own, never their sum: at most twenty-eight of either in one run. The run of the five rows makes twenty-seven sign-ins, all of them row p7's, and fourteen verifications, in rows p1 to p4, by the steps as written here; the script prints both counts before its first step.
3. **P4 reads the fact through `claim_invitations`**, which already returns `password_set`: the key on `get_user_entities` does not exist until plan 178-04. A claim with nothing to claim changes nothing.
4. **The identity-provider row of P3 is recorded on a line of its own** (`P3-IDP: set` or `P3-IDP: empty`). `empty` contradicts what the research cites and REG-11 leans on — such an account would meet the gate during its first registration — and is reported to the orchestrator before plan 178-14 runs; it is not this plan's to resolve.
5. **Single use is observed; the lifetime of a link is read and pinned, not waited out** (item PL-27, added by the revision and put to the operator). D-18 names two bounds on a mailed link, single use and `otp_expiry`, and asks that both be asserted. Row p1 presents a token twice. For the lifetime it records the value the tree states, on a line of its own; plan 178-20 pins that value in the auth-configuration gate and compares it with the running service. No step ages a link or waits an hour.

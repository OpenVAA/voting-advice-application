<objective>
End the phase's second mechanism: delete the two shims, the role table, and the two enums that only it
used — and prove the estate that was built on them still asserts what it asserted.

**Purpose.** K1 ruled one rewrite with shims and the shims deleted in-phase, because a surviving
authority table is a second mechanism whether or not anything reads it. Everything up to 162-14 added; this
plan is the only one that subtracts, and subtraction has a failure mode the additions did not: **the suite
can go green by asserting nothing.** The retired claim helper is called from most of the pgTAP estate. A
replacement that seeds fewer identities denies everyone and reddens loudly; a replacement that seeds
broader ones allows everyone and reddens nothing, because an allow-dominated estate cannot see a caller
that can do too much. So the evidence here is not the green run. It is two deliberate authority mutations
measured on the tree before the replacement and again after it, compared as sets of reddened assertion
descriptions, with the post-plan set required to contain the pre-plan one.

**The second failure mode is a database that will not build.** Two enums and one table are referenced from
places no wave has touched — a plpgsql variable declaration in the transactional-email helper among them —
and a declaration referring to a type that is not there aborts `yarn db:reset` rather than reddening a
test. The dependency census is therefore proven by reproducing that abort deliberately, capturing what it
names, and only then removing the objects in an order that does not produce it.

**The third is a safety net cut down while still holding weight.** 162-05 and 162-06 each installed a
biconditional tying a transitional object's lifetime to the thing that made it necessary. 162-06's is aimed
squarely at this commit. It is observed doing its job — red, against an intermediate tree where the table is
gone and the function that reads it is not — before it is retired, and what replaces it is strictly
stronger than what it was, because a biconditional whose two sides are both false is satisfied by both
sides coming back.

**Output.** Five objects absent from the declarative schema and from the applied database; a fixture claim
helper replaced and shown to discriminate at least as sharply as the one it replaces; the
transactional-email helper re-expressed over the grant map and proven output-identical in the one window
where that comparison exists; every dangling reference derived and dispositioned; a new structural pgTAP
file asserting the five absences from the catalogue; and the wave-6 boundary gate including the full E2E
suite.

helper across 9 of the 12 files**, naming **6 distinct fixture identities** — `candidate_a` 23,

## The design, settled before the first line of SQL

### What is removed, and what "removed" means

Five objects, none of them removed by a statement. Under § 11.3 the declarative schema is the definition of
the database and `yarn db:reset` rebuilds from it, so a removal is a deleted declaration and a regenerated
`00001_initial_schema.sql`. Nothing in this plan appends a drop.

| Object | Declared in | What goes with it |
|---|---|---|
| the role table | `300-auth-tables.sql` | its index, its row-level-security enable, its two policies, its table grant to the auth-admin role, its blanket revoke — **but not** the schema-level grant (M7) |
| the role enum | `000-enums.sql` | the header roster line describing it |
| the scope enum | `000-enums.sql` | the header roster line describing it |
| the role predicate shim | `301-auth-functions.sql` | nothing else; its signature is why it leaves with the two enums (M4) |
| the project predicate shim | `301-auth-functions.sql` | nothing else |
| the transitional backfill | `300-auth-tables.sql` | its derived call sites (162-06 says two in one place and three in another — **derive**) |

### The order, forced by M3 and M4

The role predicate's signature names both enums, and the table's columns are of both enums, and the
email helper declares a variable of one of them. So the five are a single atomic unit and the deletion is
**one commit**. What is not atomic is the preparation, and it has one correct order:

1. **The email helper moves first**, while both the retired table and the grant map carry the same eight
   identities. That window is the only one in which its output can be captured before and after and
   compared — once the table is gone there is nothing to compare against, and once the fixture stops
   writing role rows the old body returns nothing for anybody. **A differential that exists for exactly one
   task is taken in that task or not at all.**
2. **The fixture moves second**, because step 1's differential depends on the fixture still writing both.
3. **The deletion is third**, and by then nothing reads what is being deleted except the assertions whose
   retirement this plan is recording.

### The replacement for the claim helper, and why it keeps the call shape

The retired helper maps a fixture identity name to a claim array. 162-06 already changed what
`set_test_user` does with that array: the claim it sets is built from `test_grants_claim(p_user_id)` — the
same projection the access-token hook performs — and the third argument became a tripwire. **The
replacement keeps the call shape and changes the vocabulary**: `test_user_grants(user_name text)
RETURNS jsonb`, same position, same 66 call sites, one token renamed at each.

Three things that shape falls out of:

- **`create_test_data()` writes its grant rows from that one helper.** It stops writing role rows and stops
  calling the backfill, and the authority map of the fixture is written down in exactly one place — which
  is what makes a mutation of that place a measurement of the whole estate rather than of one file.
- **`set_test_user` keeps building the claim from the table projection.** 162-06's property — that all 393
  assertions run against a table-to-claim path rather than a synthesised one — is preserved, not reversed.
  What changes is the third parameter's job: it stops being a tripwire whose subject this plan deletes and
  becomes a **two-source agreement assertion**, raising a named exception when the array a call site passes
  is not set-equal to the projection of that identity's rows. Every one of the 66 call sites then performs
  that check, which is strictly more than the tripwire did.
- **The parameter's NAME changes and its identity list does not.** PostgreSQL refuses to rename an input
  parameter through `CREATE OR REPLACE`, and a stale copy from a previous run would make the replacement
  error. The declaration is therefore preceded by an explicit drop of the exact three-argument signature,
  with a comment saying why, and `pg_get_function_identity_arguments` is asserted unchanged afterwards so
  all 66 call sites still bind. **This is a test-estate file, not `schema/`** — the prohibition on drop
  statements is about the declarative schema, and `00-helpers.test.sql` already uses conditional drops.

### How the replacement is shown to still discriminate

The dangerous replacement is not the one that breaks. It is the one that seeds **more** authority than it
should, because an estate dominated by allow-assertions goes green against an identity that can do
everything. So discrimination is measured, in both directions, on both trees:

| Mutation | What it does | Which direction it probes |
|---|---|---|
| **OPEN** | every fixture identity's authority row becomes a global-admin one | fail-open: reddens the estate's denial assertions |
| **CLOSED** | no fixture identity gets an authority row at all | fail-closed: reddens the estate's allow assertions |

Each is applied to the **pre-plan** tree through the mechanism that exists there (role rows plus the
backfill) and to the **post-plan** tree through the mechanism that replaces it (grant rows written from the
helper). What is captured is not a count but the **set of reddened assertion description strings**, because
two counts can agree while the sets have moved. The gate is set containment:

> every assertion that reddened under mutation M on the pre-plan tree reddens under mutation M on the
> post-plan tree

Containment rather than equality, because the new fixture may catch strictly more. The plan **halts naming
the lost assertions** if it does not hold — that is the literal form of *take a known-failing condition the
old helper caught and observe the new one still catches it*, stated per assertion rather than in aggregate.
Both mutations are reverted and the tree is proven restored; neither is ever committed.

### The two biconditionals, and why replacing them is a strengthening

A biconditional `A ⟺ B` with both sides false **passes**, and goes on passing if both sides come back
together. After this plan that is the state of both of them: 162-05's has had both sides false since
162-06, and 162-06's gets there in this commit. They are therefore replaced, each by two unconditional
absence assertions, one per side. The replacement detects a resurrection of either half; the biconditional
detects only a resurrection of one half without the other.

That argument would justify deleting 162-06's without ceremony, and this plan does not. It is the only
assertion in the estate aimed at this commit, so it is **observed doing its job first**: an intermediate
tree is built in which the table's declaration is gone and the backfill's is not, the file carrying the
biconditional is run against it, and the assertion is captured red by its description string. The
capture, not the green run that follows, is the retirement record.

### Every other assertion whose subject this plan deletes

Retirement is a disposition, not a deletion, and the disposition set is **derived from the files as they
exist** rather than transcribed here — 162-04 through 162-14 wrote those files and may have built them
differently. The rule is 162-06's, applied to its own output:

> a deleted assertion with no successor and no recorded reason is a failure of this plan

and one thing is added to it: **a retirement citing a successor is discharged against a live successor** —
the successor file is named, its declared `plan()` count is read from the tree, and it is observed passing
in the same run. A successor cited but never looked at is a reason-shaped sentence, not a replacement.

### The email helper, re-expressed and not widened

The retired lookup asked the role table for the caller's entity context, accepting two entity kinds and
preferring the candidate one. The grant map answers the same question with `scope = 'entity'`, the entity
kind in `target_type` and the id in `target_id`. The re-expression is one `SELECT ... INTO` re-pointed, one
declared variable retyped from the retired role enum to the surviving entity-kind enum, and the same two
entity kinds in the same order.

**The two entity kinds are not widened to four.** The grant map admits factions and alliances; the retired
table had no member for either, so the retired body could never resolve one. Serving them would change what
a live email renders, inside a plan whose gate is a removal. It is recorded as a brief § 6.1 item — that
phase owns organization, faction and alliance onboarding — rather than closed here.

### Where the absences are asserted from

From the catalogue, always. `pg_class` for the relation, `pg_type` for the two enums, `pg_proc` for the
three functions at **any** signature, `pg_policies` for the two policies, `has_schema_privilege` and
`has_table_privilege` for M7's surviving grant and the grant map's continued inaccessibility. A source file
cannot satisfy or invalidate any of them, which is the property M8's comment sweep does not have and is why
that sweep is a separate, set-identity check against a reviewed deferral list rather than a grep for zero.

<objective>
Route the authority decision of 25 policies — the account tier, the project row, the three structure
tables and the two join tables — through `user_can`, and finish ROADMAP criterion 2 by making the read
predicate and the write predicate on the same object name different permissions.

**Purpose.** 162-04 proved one policy end to end and deliberately left `admin_update_projects` on the
legacy predicate so criterion 2's collapse would stay visible in the diff. This plan converts that twin and
the 24 policies around it. It is the first plan in the phase whose work is *bulk* conversion, and bulk is
where this wave's hazard lives: **a converted policy that is wrong in the permissive direction breaks
nothing, reddens nothing and ships.** A grid of read tests cannot tell "correctly allowed" from "should
have been denied". So the unit of work here is not a converted predicate, it is a converted predicate plus
the caller who must be refused by it, and the two callers of each pair differ in exactly one permission.

**Criterion 2 is a deliverable, not a side effect.** The ROADMAP asks for a pgTAP test that fails if the
two predicates ever collapse back into one. That test does not exist yet — 162-04 asserted only that the
two strings are unequal, which stays true even if both are converted onto the same permission. This plan
asserts the literals: the SELECT names the read permission and not the write one, the UPDATE names the
write permission and not the read one, on the applied database, in a pgTAP file. And it asserts the
criterion's own sentence behaviourally at the table the criterion names — `candidate_a` reads the
project's elections and cannot edit them.

**The failure this plan can cause that nothing would report.** Two of the seven tables carry no
`project_id` and are never read by a `from()` call at all: `supabaseDataProvider.ts` reaches them as
PostgREST **embedded resources** hanging off `elections` and `constituency_groups`. An embedded resource
that the policy denies comes back as an **empty array**, not an error — so an election renders with no
constituency groups and the application says nothing. That is why the join-table predicates delegate to the
parent's own policy rather than re-deriving a gate, why the delegation is the identical expression 162-08
wrote for their anon twins, and why the full E2E suite is the second instrument on this plan rather than a
formality.

**The other direction, stated so it is not dropped.** Three of these policies carry a public disjunct
today. Removing it would make an authenticated caller with no grant see strictly less than an anon caller —
a logged-in user staring at a blank voter application — and it would redden an existing assertion that
calls the behaviour `by design`. The disjunct is re-expressed against 162-08's `project_open_for_voters`
helper rather than dropped, and both terms of the disjunction are measured alone.

**Output.** Two hardened hop helpers in `301-auth-functions.sql`; 25 converted predicates in `302-rls.sql`;
a new pgTAP file carrying 25 paired cases, the criterion-2 structural and behavioural pairs, five
anon-parity biconditionals and six table-scoped absence scans; four recorded wrong-predicate control runs;
two existing test files re-expressed without a description string moving; a regenerated
`00001_initial_schema.sql` and `packages/supabase-types/`; and the wave-4 gate including the full E2E suite.
</objective>

## Measured facts this plan is built on

Ten measurements taken in this worktree on 2026-09-16 against branch `integration/ship-12-squash`, HEAD
`df719df28`, all of them from tracked source. **Each is re-derived by a verify block below rather than
trusted** — the ones that matter most are M1, M5 and M6.

**M1 — the policy census, derived by parsing the file.** `302-rls.sql` declares **80** policies. **31** are
on this plan's seven tables: `accounts` 4, `projects` 4, `elections` 5, `constituency_groups` 5,
`constituencies` 5, `constituency_group_constituencies` 4, `election_constituency_groups` 4. Of the 31,
**five** are `TO anon … FOR SELECT` and are 162-08's; **one**, `authenticated_select_projects`, is 162-04's;
**25 remain and are this plan's.** The partition of the remaining 49 is exact and is the check that the
seven-table set is the right one: 162-10's four entity tables carry 22, 162-11's five carry 22, and
162-12's `nominations` carries 5.

**M2 — the predicate occurrences, per policy, with comments stripped.** Across the 31: `can_access_project`
23, `has_role` 10, `published` 6, `USING (true)` 4. 162-04 removed one of each of the first two with
`authenticated_select_projects`; 162-08 removed three `published` and two `USING (true)` with the five anon
policies. **The state this plan inherits is therefore 22, 8, 3 and 2, and all four go to zero.** File-wide
afterwards, `can_access_project` stands at **44** and `has_role` at **4**, all of them on 162-10, 162-11 and
162-12's blocks — which is why every negative scan in this plan reads `pg_policies` filtered to the seven
tables and never greps the file. A file-wide absence gate here would be unsatisfiable against a sibling's
correct code.

**M3 — where each of criterion 2's two sentences lands.** The criterion is written twice over. Its
*structural* sentence is about `projects`: the SELECT and the UPDATE were the same predicate, 162-04
separated them, and this plan gives the UPDATE its own permission per D-09 so the separation is a statement
about authority rather than about which plan got there first. Its *behavioural* sentence names a different
table — *"a candidate can read the project's elections and cannot edit them"* — and lands on
`authenticated_select_elections` against `admin_update_elections`. Both are this plan's, and they are
asserted independently rather than one being taken as evidence for the other.

**M4 — the legacy predicate's translations are settled, so every conversion is a measured population
change.** 162-05 established `can_access_project(X)` as exactly `user_can('project', X,
'project.edit_project_settings')`, and translated the three executable `has_role` shapes:
`has_role('super_admin')` at global scope with a NULL target, and `has_role('account_admin','account',X)` at
account scope. Eight of the ten `has_role` occurrences on these tables are those two shapes; the four that
survive this plan are the `organization` shape in 162-10's blocks. **The global-scope-with-NULL-target idiom
this plan uses for the two root-only account policies is 162-05's, not a new one.**

**M5 — ⚠ the disjunct that keeps a logged-in caller from seeing less than a logged-out one.** The three
authenticated structure SELECT policies carry a public disjunct today, `01-tenant-isolation.test.sql`
Section 1 asserts it with the description string `admin_b CAN see published elections from Project A (by
design)`, and the file's own header states it as a key principle. 162-08 re-expressed the anon half against
`project_open_for_voters` and its prohibitions hand the authenticated half here by name. ROADMAP criterion 5
says *a published project is readable by anyone*, and **anyone** includes an authenticated caller.
Dropping the disjunct would redden that existing assertion, would deny a freshly-identified user everything
until their grant exists, and would put a logged-in user in front of a blank voter application.

**M6 — ⚠ the two join tables are read as PostgREST embedded resources and by nothing else.**
`supabaseDataProvider.ts` issues `elections` selected with `election_constituency_groups(constituency_group_id)`
embedded and `constituency_groups` selected with `constituency_group_constituencies(constituency_id)`
embedded; a tracked-source sweep for a `from()` call on either table returns **zero**. An embedded resource
is filtered by the embedded table's own RLS and a denial yields an **empty array, not an error** — so
`constituencyGroupIds` and `constituencyIds` come back empty, an election renders with no constituency
groups, and nothing anywhere reports it. This is the one failure in this plan that produces no message.

**M7 — the fixture has no project-editor identity, and a ninth cannot be added where the others live.**
`create_test_data()` inserts eight `auth.users` rows and eight role rows — two project admins, three
candidates, one organization, one global admin, one account admin — and 162-06 asserts the fixture produces
exactly **8** grant rows and that a second backfill call inserts zero. A project-scope `editor` grant is the
one caller that separates `project.edit_structure` from `project.edit_project_settings`, so this plan
creates it, and its `auth.users` row, **inside its own pgTAP transaction**, which rolls back with the file.

**M8 — the existing assertions on these seven tables, and why each survives.** `04-admin-crud.test.sql`

### Why the read half of rows 8-10 is a two-term disjunction and not one `user_can` call

**A single authority call is the wrong shape here and M5 is the measurement that says so.** The disjunct is
not a second authority mechanism: `project_open_for_voters` answers a **row-state** question, and 162-04's
Task 2 Q3 ratified that row state belongs to the policies that call `user_can`, never to the matrix inside
it. So the policy asks two different questions of two different instruments — *may this caller* of
`user_can`, and *is this row public* of 162-08's helper — and takes the disjunction. That is the same shape
the policy has today, with both halves modernised, which is what keeps the diff reviewable.

The alternative — dropping the public half — is the "too strict" failure, and it is loud rather than
silent: an existing assertion reddens, the E2E voter specs go dark for any logged-in caller, and a
freshly-identified user is denied everything between `identity-callback` and their grant row. It is not the
dangerous direction, but it is the one that would waste a wave, so it is measured alone rather than
reasoned about: one assertion reads a closed project through the authority term with the public term false,
and one reads an open project through the public term with the authority term false.

### Why the two join tables delegate instead of asking

Neither carries a `project_id`, and both carry `constituency_group_id`. 162-08 gave their anon SELECT
policies an `EXISTS` over `constituency_groups` and stated the reason: the sub-select is evaluated under the
caller's own row-level security, so it resolves to *the parent is visible to you*, which is the parent's
policy answering rather than a second copy of the rule. **This plan writes the identical expression for the
authenticated twin**, so the two roles cannot drift apart, and so a later change to the parent's visibility
reaches the join table without anyone remembering to make it. There is no cycle: the parent's policy does
not reference the join tables.

This is the one place an inline sub-select is correct on these seven tables, it is correct because it
delegates to a policy rather than re-deriving a predicate, and it is the named exception in the structural
assertion that every other converted predicate is a single `user_can` call.

### Why the four join-table writes take a helper instead of the same `EXISTS`

A write must not be gated by a sub-select the caller's own row-level security filters — that makes the
answer depend on the caller's *read* access to the parent as well as on their authority over it, which is
two questions where the phase wants one. The hop therefore moves into a `SECURITY DEFINER` helper, exactly
as `entity_project_id` does for the entity tables, and the predicate becomes a single `user_can` call whose
target is the helper's result.

| Function | Signature | Reads | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `public.constituency_group_project_id` | `(p_constituency_group_id uuid) RETURNS uuid` | `constituency_groups`, by primary key | which project owns this group |
| `public.election_project_id` | `(p_election_id uuid) RETURNS uuid` | `elections`, by primary key | which project owns this election |

Both are `LANGUAGE sql STABLE SECURITY DEFINER SET search_path = ''`, both return NULL when the lookup finds
no row, and **NULL is a denial rather than a hole**: 162-04 asserts that `user_can` answers false for a NULL
target at project scope. That is asserted here directly, on a join row naming a parent that does not exist,
rather than inferred from three-valued logic.

**These are two named functions and not one generic one, and that is not a D-21 violation.** D-21's
instruction is that no policy names a single *entity type* where it could take one as an argument; entity
types are an enum with four members and a shared shape. `constituency_groups` and `elections` are two
different tables with two different primary keys and no enum relating them, and the generic alternative is
dynamic SQL inside a security predicate. The generalisation D-21 asks for is applied where it applies —
rows 8-19 name no table in their predicate at all.

### Where the enum has no member for the question a policy asks

**§ 3.2 enumerates no account-read permission.** The Account group has exactly three members and all three
carry the identical column pattern in § 3.3 — granted to the root admin and to the account admin, to nobody
else — so the choice between them changes no population at any scope. Rows 1 and 3 therefore name the same
literal, and that is the matrix's construction rather than a read/write collapse: the criterion-2 rule is
asserted over `projects`, `elections`, `constituency_groups` and `constituencies`, and `accounts` is
excluded **by name, with the reason recorded**, rather than by the rule quietly not covering it. The gap is
a finding for `162-SPEC.md` and 162-17, not something a wave-4 plan closes by adding an enum member.

**Rows 2 and 4 ask at global scope with a NULL target, and the reason is that account scope would be
wrong in the dangerous direction.** A `WITH CHECK` on an account insert evaluates against the new row, so
an account-scope ask would be answered by any grant the caller happens to hold on that uuid — and
`grants.target_id` has no foreign key to `accounts`, so a grant on an id that does not exist yet is a legal
row. An account admin granted on a uuid nobody has used could then create that account and own it. The
global-scope form has no such path, preserves today's behaviour exactly, and is 162-05's own translation of
the root-admin check.

### The caller set the grid is built on

Seven identities, six of them the fixture's and one created inside the test file's transaction (M7). Each
paired assertion names two of them that differ in exactly the permission under test.

| Identity | Grant | Holds | Does not hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| `super_admin` | `global` / `admin` | everything | — |
| `account_admin_a` | `account` account_a / `admin` | `account.*`, and everything below it in project A | — |
| `admin_a` | `project` project_a / `admin` | `project.edit_project_settings`, `project.edit_structure` | `account.manage_projects` |
| a project editor of A | `project` project_a / `editor` | `project.edit_structure`, `project.read_structure` | `project.edit_project_settings`, `project.manage_editors` |
| `candidate_a` | `entity` candidate / `editor` | `project.read_structure` | `project.edit_structure` |
| `admin_b` | `project` project_b / `admin` | the same, in project B | anything in project A |
| no grants at all | — | — | everything; sees only what anon sees |

The three read/write pairs fall straight out of the last two columns: `candidate_a` versus itself across
SELECT and UPDATE on `elections` is criterion 2's sentence; the project editor versus itself across SELECT
and UPDATE on `projects` is criterion 2's structure; `admin_a` versus `account_admin_a` across UPDATE and
DELETE on `projects` is row 7's narrowing.

### What the grid has to prove that a read test cannot

1. **The caller who may, may.** Necessary, and satisfied on its own by the ungated form these two join
   tables carry today.
2. **The caller who may not, may not — and differs in exactly one permission.** One pair per policy.
3. **Each converted permission is independently load-bearing.** Property 2 still passes if two permissions
   happen to be held by the same identities, so the grid is run against four **deliberately wrong**
   predicate sets before the real ones are accepted, and each run's reddened count is recorded: the read
   verb substituted for the write verb on the elections quad; criterion 2's collapse reintroduced on
   `admin_update_projects`; the public disjunct dropped from rows 8-10; and the join-table SELECT left
   ungated. Two of the four redden the may-not half and two the may half, so the grid is shown to measure
   in both directions rather than merely to pass.

### What this plan does not decide

`is_child_nominee`'s read role — § 3.4's third row, *an entity grantee reads a related entity's basic data
through the child hop* — belongs to 162-10 and 162-12, on the tables that carry entities and nominations.
None of this plan's seven tables holds entity data, so the child hop has no call site here and none is
invented. ROADMAP criterion 5's second clause is likewise split: this plan delivers *an unpublished
project's non-entities are readable by any grantee*, and *its entities by the grantees themselves and their
parents* is 162-10's. The SUMMARY states the split, because a criterion recorded as satisfied when two
thirds of it is built is how a phase closes early.

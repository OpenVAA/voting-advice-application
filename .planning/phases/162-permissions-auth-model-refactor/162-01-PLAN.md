## Phase decision record (canonical — cited by this plan's checkpoint)

### DR-1 — The role vocabulary is two levels, and that overrules a document marked *locked*

`162-CONTEXT.md` D-02 fixes the vocabulary at `admin` and `editor`. Two standing documents say three:

- **ROADMAP § Phase 162, criterion 1** — "roles `admin` / `owner` / `editor` carrying the documented
  rights — admin does anything to the object and its descendants, **owner manages editors**, editor has
  owner rights minus editor management." The same three-role sentence is the PRESHIP-02 row in
  `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md`, a requirement the source document marks **blocking ship**.
- **`.planning/v2.15-OPERATOR-DECISIONS-2026-08-29.md` § K4**, which the CONTEXT's canonical-references
  section lists as **locked**: "Level-1 is defined as **editor minus editor-management** — criterion 1
  already describes editor as 'owner rights minus editor management', so the definition follows."

The brief's § 11.9 records ruling 8.1 as resolved by **★ default** — that is, by the standing convention
that an unticked box selects its recommended option. There is no explicit operator tick on it and no
free-text note. The amendment is therefore carried by a convention, while the thing it overrules is
carried by a document marked locked and by a blocking-ship requirement. That asymmetry is what the
checkpoint in task 1 puts in front of the operator, once, before the vocabulary becomes normative.

The argument for two levels, stated so the operator is ratifying a position rather than a preference:
`162-USER-RIGHTS.md` names eight user types and they span `admin` and `editor` only; editor-management
is the project-scope permission `project.manage_editors` and the entity-scope equivalent is
`entity.invite_children`, so no third level carries anything; and an unused `owner` member in a security
enum is untested surface.

### DR-2 — K4's single subtraction becomes three, and the SPEC says so

K4 defines level-1 as editor minus editor-management — **one** subtraction. The brief's § 3.3 defines
`ProjectEditor` as `ProjectAdmin` minus **three** permissions: `project.manage_editors`,
`project.edit_project_settings` (added by § 11.1's split, which is D-09) and `nomination.confirm`
(which `162-USER-RIGHTS.md` restricts to admins). § 6 of the SPEC states this widening explicitly and
names both authorities, so a later reader does not have to reconcile two definitions by guessing which
is newer.

### DR-3 — What this SPEC deliberately does not carry

The brief's **§ 3.5 (where each new setting goes)** is **not** reproduced in this SPEC, and the omission
is deliberate rather than an oversight. `162-CONTEXT.md` D-16 resolves the nomination-shape setting as a
new `elections.nomination_shape` column with `elections.election_type` left alone, citing brief § 8.2(a)
and § 11.9's "★ default" row — while the body of brief § 8.2 carries an unticked-box free-text operator
note in bold reading *"Reuse election_type for this because the current general, local is not actually

opposite instruction. `162-DISCUSSION-POINTS.md` mentions neither column, so the brief's note is the only
operator text on the question and nothing reconciles it with D-16.

That contradiction belongs to **162-07**, which creates the column. Reproducing § 3.5 here would

<objective>
Route the seventeen convertible policies on `questions`, `question_categories`, `app_settings`, `feedback`
and `admin_jobs` through `user_can` — and make § 11.1's split between the two settings permissions a
measured difference between two identities rather than a sentence in a schema file.

**Purpose.** These five tables are where the phase's central claim gets its least visible test. Three of
them — `questions`, `question_categories`, `app_settings` — are the application's entire content and
configuration read surface, so a predicate that is too strict blanks the product and the E2E suite says so
at the first spec. The other two are the opposite case: **nothing in the E2E suite reads or deletes
`feedback`, and nothing in it touches `admin_jobs` at all.** A widened policy on either is invisible —
`feedback` carries user-submitted content, and `admin_jobs` carries the initiating operator's address and
the LLM job's prompt and response. Their assertions have to carry the whole weight, so every one of them is
paired: one caller who may, one who may not, differing in exactly the permission under test.

**§ 11.1 is the plan's point, and it is the thing a single-identity test cannot see.** D-09 splits one
permission in two along the line *does a policy read this value*: `project.edit_project_settings` governs
`projects` and is admin-only; `project.edit_app_settings` governs `app_settings` and editors hold it too.
The two are granted to the same people in six of § 3.3's seven columns — every column except
`ProjectEditor`. So a test run as an admin passes whichever literal is written, and a test run as a
candidate fails whichever literal is written. **The split is observable only against an identity that holds
one and not the other, and no such identity exists anywhere in the tree**: `create_test_data()` has eight,
and not one of them is a project editor. This plan builds it, and then proves the split is load-bearing by
swapping the two literals and watching the editor's allow-case go red.

**One pair cannot be separated by any identity, and that is stated rather than hidden.** `feedback.read`
and `feedback.manage` are granted to exactly the same four roles in § 3.3, so no behavioural test
distinguishes them today. The separation is structural — a policy-to-permission map derived from the
applied database — and the plan measures the limit honestly: the literals are swapped, the behavioural grid
is observed to stay green, and the structural assertion is observed to redden. That is what makes the map an
instrument rather than a decoration.

**Output.** Seventeen converted predicates in `302-rls.sql` with the comments that explain them; a new
pgTAP file carrying the § 11.1 grid, the read grid, thirteen paired write assertions and the structural
map; four planted-variant runs with their reddened counts; two existing test files' stale comments
corrected; a regenerated `00001_initial_schema.sql`; and a full E2E suite.
</objective>

## The design, settled before the first line of SQL

### The three-way partition of the 22

Every policy on the five tables falls into exactly one set, and the SUMMARY reports all three sizes.

| Set | Size | Members | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **162-08's** | 3 | `anon_select_questions`, `anon_select_question_categories`, `anon_select_app_settings` | `TO anon` SELECT. 162-08 gated all thirteen of them on the project-open helper; the third was one of its three ungated `USING (true)` cases. Byte-identical here. |
| **Converted here** | 17 | the three `authenticated_select_*`, the nine admin CRUD policies on `questions` / `question_categories` / `app_settings`, `admin_select_feedback`, `admin_delete_feedback`, and the three `admin_*_admin_jobs` | every one of them today asks a question `user_can` answers |
| **Deliberately ungated** | 2 | `anon_insert_feedback`, `authenticated_insert_feedback` | submitting feedback is not a member of § 3.2, and § 3.2 is canonical |

`3 + 17 + 2 = 22`, and the executor derives all four numbers rather than reading them here.

### The seventeen predicates

The read form and the write form, written once and instantiated seventeen times. `<open>` is the
project-open helper 162-08 declared; its exact name, schema qualification and argument spelling are read
from the applied database at Task 1 and from the form 162-09 and 162-10 already committed in this same
file, so that one file does not end up carrying two styles.

| Policies | Command | New expression |
|---|---|---|
| `authenticated_select_questions` · `_question_categories` · `_app_settings` | SELECT (`USING`) | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, 'project.read_structure')) OR (SELECT <open> (project_id))` |
| `admin_insert_questions` · `_update_` · `_delete_` and the three `question_categories` twins | INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, 'project.edit_questions'))` |
| `admin_insert_app_settings` · `admin_update_app_settings` · `admin_delete_app_settings` | INSERT / UPDATE / DELETE | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, 'project.edit_app_settings'))` — **§ 11.1** |
| `admin_select_feedback` | SELECT | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, 'feedback.read'))` |
| `admin_delete_feedback` | DELETE | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, 'feedback.manage'))` |
| `admin_select_admin_jobs` · `admin_insert_admin_jobs` · `admin_delete_admin_jobs` | SELECT / INSERT / DELETE | `(SELECT user_can ('project', project_id, '<the verb Task 2 ratifies>'))` |

House conventions the file's own header states and all 80 policies obey, preserved exactly: SELECT is
`USING` only, INSERT is `WITH CHECK` only, UPDATE is both with the **same** expression on each side,
DELETE is `USING` only; `TO anon` or `TO authenticated` always named; the predicate always wrapped
`(SELECT …)` so `auth.jwt()` is evaluated once per query rather than once per row.

**Why the read policies keep a disjunct.** § 3.4's second row — *"any auth user can always read their
project"* — is the first term: `project.read_structure` is ✓ in all seven columns of § 3.3, and 162-04's
project-read branch makes an entity grant answer yes to it at project scope for its own project, so the
call means exactly *the caller holds some grant in this project*. It does **not** cover an authenticated
caller with no grant in the project — a logged-in candidate of one project browsing another's voter app,
which is what the retired per-row flag covers today. Without the second term those callers would see
nothing at all, and `_getAppSettings` would throw rather than render. The disjunct is the authenticated
mirror of the rule 162-08 wrote for anon, and it is strictly less permissive than the term it replaces.

**And the disjunct is a helper call, not a lookup.** D-15 put `open_for_voters` on `projects` as a typed
column *because RLS reads it*, and `public.projects` carries RLS of its own — 162-08 measured that an
inline `EXISTS … FROM public.projects` inside a policy returns zero rows for every caller and that the
working form is a `SECURITY DEFINER` helper. That measurement is consumed here, not repeated: no policy
this plan writes contains a sub-select over `public.projects`. The same line of D-15's reasoning is what
makes § 11.1's split a split at all — the column whose value these three predicates read is governed by
`project.edit_project_settings`, and the permission this plan grants editors, `project.edit_app_settings`,
deliberately does not reach it.

**Why `admin_jobs` takes one verb for all three commands, if Task 2 ratifies the recommendation.** The
enum has no read verb whose holder set fits. The only read-shaped member is `project.read_structure`, which
is ✓ for all four entity columns — using it on `admin_select_admin_jobs` would hand every candidate in the
project the operator's address and the LLM job's prompt and response, and would redden the estate's
existing `candidate_a cannot SELECT admin_jobs (admin-only table)` assertion. So the read/write split this
phase installs elsewhere is **deliberately not made here**, and the reason is recorded rather than left to
look like an omission.

### The evidence, and what each instrument can and cannot see

Three properties, and only the first is what a passing policy test measures.

1. **The caller who may, does.** Necessary, and on its own satisfied by `USING (true)`.
2. **The caller who may not, does not — and differs from the first in exactly the permission under test.**
   One pair per converted policy. For the settings pair the difference is carried by an identity
   (`ProjectEditor` versus `ProjectAdmin`) across two tables; for every other policy it is carried by an
   entity grantee of the same project, who holds `project.read_structure` and none of the write verbs.
3. **The permission literal is the one the plan says it is.** Property 2 cannot see this when two
   permissions are coextensive (M6), so a structural map derived from `pg_policies` states the
   policy-to-literal correspondence directly, and the planted-variant runs measure exactly where each
   instrument stops.

**The four planted variants, each with a recorded reddened count and a stated direction:**

| Variant | Expected to redden | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| every converted expression replaced by `true` | the deny half, in bulk | the grid measures denial at all |
| every converted expression `AND false` | the allow half, in bulk | the grid measures permission at all |
| `project.edit_app_settings` → `project.edit_project_settings` on `admin_update_app_settings` | **exactly the editor allow-cases** | § 11.1's split is load-bearing, not decorative |
| `feedback.read` ↔ `feedback.manage` swapped | **the structural map only; the behavioural grid stays green** | M6 measured rather than argued — the limit of the behavioural instrument, recorded |

### Why the hold on the post did not reach the wait: the click waits for the navigation it starts

CLICK_WAITS_FOR_NAVIGATION=yes
    The plan's assumption that "a click does not wait for the navigation it starts" does not hold in the installed Playwright 1.63.0. `node_modules/playwright-core/lib/coreBundle.js` passes `{ ...options, waitAfter: !options.noWaitAfter }` from the click of a locator (`handle._click(progress3, { ...options, waitAfter: !options.noWaitAfter`) and of an element handle (`this._click(progress2, { ...options, waitAfter: !options.noWaitAfter })`), so `_retryPointerAction` calls `waitForSignalsCreatedBy(progress2, options.waitAfter === true, …)` with `true` by default. With it, a `SignalBarrier` is retained for each main-frame navigation the click schedules and released on that frame's first public `InternalNavigation` event, and the click logs `waiting for scheduled navigations to finish`. The `=== true` test excludes only the `"disabled"` value that hover, tap and the internal clicks pass.
    The sign-in form is `<form id={formId} method="POST" novalidate use:enhance={handleSignIn}` (apps/frontend/src/routes/contestant/login/email/+page.svelte). Before the app has started, a click on its submit button is a native document post (the annotation's `navigation=true`); after, `use:enhance` sends the post with `fetch` and the address changes through a client navigation.

### What this changes (UNCONFIRMED)

The helper's wait only sees the part of the sign-in chain that is still in flight when the click returns. With the click waiting for the navigation it starts, a slow chain is normally spent inside the click, which has no timeout of its own below the test's 90 s, and the wait then passes at once, as in both attempts. The unplanned red therefore needs a click that returned while the chain was still in flight: its call log (`waiting for "http://localhost:5273/contestant/login/email" navigation to finish...`) and its `Received string: ""` are those of the `noWaitAfter` and same-document-history probes. What released the click there is not established: a same-document history entry during the post (SvelteKit's start calls `replaceState`), or the navigation signal reaching Playwright after the click's barrier had already resolved on a host at load 19 to 29, are candidates, and neither is reproduced in the app.

The raised budget would still have passed the unplanned red if its chain finished within 30 s of the click's return. But the plan's reproduction (a hold on any hop of the chain delays the moment the wait watches) does not stand, and the same premise underlies Task 2's classes and representatives: a wait after a click that starts a document navigation is mostly covered by the click, while a wait after a client navigation (SvelteKit's `__data.json` fetch and `pushState`) is, by the same code, not waited for by the click; that last point is read from the code and not probed.

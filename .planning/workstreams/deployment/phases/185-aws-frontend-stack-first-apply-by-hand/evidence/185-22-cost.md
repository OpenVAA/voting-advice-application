### The inventory's assumptions, and what was read against them

- **One task, 0.5 vCPU and 1 GB, all month.** The service's floor is one task; a scale-out to two
  (Step 12) or a rollout's overlap adds Fargate hours the inventory leaves out.
- **Three public IPv4 addresses.** Read, not assumed: the stack's VPC holds three network interfaces
  with a public address, the task's (requester the account) and two of the load balancer's
  (requester `amazon-elb`), which confirms research A11 (two for the load balancer in two zones).
- **One LCU, an upper bound.** CloudWatch `ConsumedLCUs` for the hour of the re-apply (18:00Z to
  19:00Z), with the agent's reads and the probes in it, summed to about 0.001 LCU-hours, so the
  5.54 line over-reads idle staging by about 5.5 USD. It stays in the figure as the bound for a
  modestly used staging.
- **1 GB of logs, an upper bound.** `IncomingBytes` of `/openvaa/staging/frontend` read 141 559
  bytes on 2026-11-13 and 299 462 bytes on 2026-11-14, days full of observations (rollouts,
  restores, scaling), so well under 0.01 GB a day. The log class is `STANDARD`, retention 90 days.
- **CloudFront, an upper bound.** CloudFront's free tier is not subtracted. The hour of the
  re-apply read 85 requests and about 0.6 MB downloaded.
- **Alarms.** The two `TargetTracking-service/openvaa-staging/openvaa-staging-frontend-Alarm*`
  alarms that target tracking creates (standard resolution, 60 s period). They are owned by
  Application Auto Scaling and carry no `Root` tag, so the tag-filtered read does not see them.
  CloudWatch's free tier of ten alarms is not subtracted.
- **Left out.** The hosted zone and its queries, which are `dns-mail`'s; the image registry and
  the state bucket, which are bootstrap's; the certificate (ACM public certificates are free); the
  topic's mail and the EventBridge rule's events (a handful a month, fractions of a cent).
